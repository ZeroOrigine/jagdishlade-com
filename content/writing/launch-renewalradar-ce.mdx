**A license doesn't go inactive with a warning siren. It goes inactive with silence.**

You find out when a client asks you for proof, or a renewal fee bounces, or you go to log a hire and the portal just says no. By then the CE hours you needed are three months expired and the state doesn't care that you were slammed.

## The person I built this for

You hold more than one license. Maybe it's insurance in two states, maybe it's a nursing license plus a certification, maybe it's a CA membership plus a specialty credential. Each one has its own renewal date, its own hour requirement, its own rules about what counts and what doesn't. One state wants ethics hours split a certain way. Another doesn't care as long as the total hits. You took a course from one provider last year, a webinar from another the year before. Nobody hands you a single number that says "you're at 14 of 24, and you have until March."

So you keep it in your head, or in a spreadsheet you update twice a year, or you just trust that you'll notice. Most of the time you do notice. Once, you don't. And once is the whole problem, because a license isn't like a missed gym day. It's your right to earn income in that field.

## The wider thing this is really about

I spent years as a Chartered Accountant reconciling books. The lesson that never left me: the account doesn't lie, but the person keeping it up to date does, out of exhaustion, not dishonesty. Deadlines don't fail because people are careless. They fail because tracking them lives in too many places at once. A due date in your calendar, a rule in a PDF you downloaded once, hours scattered across two or three providers' portals that don't talk to each other.

The fix was never "try harder to remember." It was always "put it in one place that watches for you." That's true of expense reconciliation. It's true of tax filing deadlines. It's true of CE hours. Same shape of problem, different industry.

## What RenewalRadar CE actually removes

It's a state-rule database paired with a personal CE hour tracker, plus a countdown alert for each license you hold. You log your hours as you earn them, from whichever provider you actually used, because it's agnostic to that. It doesn't care if your ethics course came from a national association or a local one-day seminar. It just needs the hours and the date.

The database knows the rules per state, so it's not asking you to also become an expert in licensing law on top of your actual job. And the countdown alert per license means you're not the one responsible for noticing that March is closer than it looks. That's the step it removes: the mental math of "how many hours, by when, for which license, and did that last course even count."

It doesn't file anything for you. It doesn't take the course for you. It just keeps the one ledger you actually need, so the news reaches you a message early, not a locked portal too late.
