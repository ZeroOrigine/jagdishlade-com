Most apps built for children are built to extract something from the parent, not to give something to the child.

## The 11pm app store scroll

You know the moment. Your kid can't sleep, or won't stop asking, or you just want ten quiet minutes, so you open the app store. Every third result wants an email before it shows you a single screen. The "free" ones have a pop-up ad between levels. The good-looking ones need an account, which means a password, which means you're now the one managing your five-year-old's login credentials at 11pm.

None of this is about your child. It's about a funnel. The game is the bait, the account is the hook, the ads are the return on investment. You feel it even if you can't name it. Something in you says: this isn't for her, this is for them.

## What an account is actually a promise of

I spent years as a Chartered Accountant. My whole training was in reconciliation, every number has to match to something real, or it doesn't belong on the ledger. An account, in that world, is a record of truth. It exists so nothing gets lost and nothing gets invented.

Somewhere along the way, tech borrowed the word "account" and emptied it of that meaning. Now an account exists so a company doesn't lose *you*. It's not reconciling anything. It's just making sure there's a hook in you that doesn't come out.

A five-year-old doesn't need to be reconciled to a database. She needs a story that respects her enough to let her leave it behind the second she's done, with nothing left on a server, nothing synced, nothing sold.

## Thirty-eight dreamers, one black hole, no login

The Whole You is a free 3D dream adventure, made for ages 5 and up. It was built by someone named Advik. There are 53 chapters. Along the way there are 38 dreamers, a black hole, a castle, a paper moon. The idea underneath all of it is simple enough to say in one line: every dream shows you a piece of yourself.

There's no account to create. No ad breaks interrupting the castle. No login screen between your child and the black hole. Progress stays on the device it's played on, nowhere else. You close the tab, it remembers where you were. You don't close anything else on your end: no email, no password, no "are you sure you want to delete your data" ritual, because there was never any data to give away in the first place.

That's the whole removal. Not a new feature bolted onto the old model. Just the old model's extraction layer taken out entirely. The dream adventure is still there, 53 chapters of it, dreamers and a moon and a castle. What's missing is everything that wasn't the adventure to begin with.
