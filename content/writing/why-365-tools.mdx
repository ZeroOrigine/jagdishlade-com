The idea sounds absurd when you say it out loud. One micro-SaaS product per day, every day, for an entire year. Three hundred and sixty-five deployments. Three hundred and sixty-five Stripe integrations. Three hundred and sixty-five times asking: does anyone actually need this?

But the number isn't the point.

## The Constraint is the Teacher

When you have one day to ship, you can't overthink. You can't add features "just in case." You can't spend three weeks debating database schemas. The constraint forces clarity. What is the smallest thing that solves a real problem for a real person?

Most products fail not because they lack features, but because they lack focus. They try to be everything for everyone and end up being nothing for no one. A one-day constraint eliminates this failure mode entirely. You ship the core. You ship the truth of the product.

## What AI Actually Changes

I'm not building these alone. ZeroOrigine is an ecosystem of seven AI Minds — each with deep cognitive architecture, each with a specific role. Research. Build. Test. Market. Review ethics. Learn from outcomes. Dispatch the next project.

This is what AI collaboration actually looks like. Not "I asked ChatGPT to write my code." It's an institution. Agents with skills, principles, and judgment. My role is oversight — about seven minutes per day of real human decision-making. The rest is orchestrated.

People talk about AI replacing humans. I'm more interested in what happens when AI amplifies a single human's ability to build. What can one person with the right system create?

## 365 Hypotheses

Every micro-SaaS is a hypothesis. "I believe this specific group of people has this specific problem, and they would pay this specific amount for this specific solution." Ship it. See if anyone cares. That is the experiment.

Most will fail. That's the point. I'm not looking for a 100% hit rate. I'm looking for the handful that resonate — the ones where the hypothesis was right, where the problem was real, where someone opens their wallet and says "yes, this."

The ones that work get nurtured. The ones that don't get honest data about why. Both are valuable. Neither is wasted.

## Starting from Zero

I wrote a philosophy manuscript about going back to zero — stripping away conditioned thinking to see clearly. That's what this project is. No assumptions about what the market wants. No borrowed wisdom about product-market fit. Just build, ship, observe, learn.

365 tools. 365 chances to see something clearly. 365 days of going back to zero.

Day one was TEF Master — a French exam prep tool. Revenue: $0. Learning: immense.

Let's see what day 365 looks like.
