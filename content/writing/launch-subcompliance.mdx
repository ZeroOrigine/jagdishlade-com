A lapsed certificate has nothing to do with the quality of your work. It ends jobs anyway.

I'm thinking of the solo electrician, the framer, the HVAC tech running their own crew. You are good at your trade. You are not a compliance department. But every general contractor you work under wants a current certificate of insurance on file, and some want three or four documents behind it: additional insured endorsements, workers' comp, a signed waiver. You are juggling this across five, six, maybe ten GC relationships, each with its own renewal date, its own broker, its own inbox that never replies fast enough.

Nobody tells you the certificate expired. You find out when you show up and the site supervisor says you're not cleared to work today.

Here's the wider truth I keep running into, from tax season to construction sites: the work almost never fails first. The paperwork behind the work fails first, quietly, and then it takes the work down with it. As a CA I spent years reconciling ledgers where the numbers were fine and the filing was the problem. Trade contractors live the same trap, just with insurance instead of invoices. The job is solid. The document trail isn't. And the trail is what gets checked.

The fix isn't a better calendar or another app you have to remember to open. The fix is removing the step where you have to remember at all.

SubCompliance tracks every general contractor relationship you have and watches the clock on each certificate. It warns you before anything lapses, not after. And when it's time to renew, it drafts the actual request to your broker, the one you'd normally sit down and write yourself after a long day on site. You review it, you send it, you're covered. That's the step it removes: the remembering, the drafting, the gap between "I should get to that" and it actually getting done.
