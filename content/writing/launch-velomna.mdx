**You already dream next to strangers every night. You just never see the room.**

I want to talk to the person who writes something true at 1am and then deletes it before posting. You know the one. A half-formed thought, a dream fragment, a sentence that's too honest for the feed you're about to put it on. You write it, you reread it, and you think: if I post this with my name on it, someone will ask me about it tomorrow. So you delete it. The true thing goes nowhere. It just evaporates.

## The problem isn't the thought. It's the name attached to it.

Here's what I've noticed, watching people online and watching myself: almost nothing stops us from being honest except the fact that honesty is traceable. Put your name on a thought and it becomes a position. Something you have to defend, explain, or live up to tomorrow. So most of what's true in a person never gets written down at all. It just gets carried, privately, until it fades or turns into something smaller and safer.

I spent years as a Chartered Accountant before any of this. Ledgers, reconciliations, numbers that had to match on both sides or the whole thing was wrong. Here's the thing nobody tells you about a ledger: it doesn't care who you are. A debit from a sole proprietor and a debit from a multinational sit in the same column, doing the same job. The ledger only asks one question: does this balance? Identity is irrelevant to whether something is true.

I think that's the thing we lost somewhere between double-entry bookkeeping and the modern feed. We built systems where identity matters more than the truth of the entry. Where who said it outweighs what was said. And that trade has a cost: it makes honesty expensive, so people stop paying it.
