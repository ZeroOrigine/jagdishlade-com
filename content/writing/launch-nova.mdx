Most "free" homework apps aren't free. They want your email first. Then your kid's name. Then a credit card "just to verify you're not a robot." By the time the actual help arrives, the homework is due tomorrow and everyone's given up.

## The scene I keep seeing

It's 9pm. There's a worksheet on the table, or a half-written essay, or a block of code that won't run. Your kid is stuck. You want to help, but it's been twenty years since you did algebra, or you've never touched Python, and you can feel the frustration building on both sides of the table.

So you look for an AI tool. And you hit a wall of forms. Sign up. Verify your email. Pick a plan. Enter a card "you won't be charged yet." All before a single question gets answered. The kid has lost interest by the second screen.

I've sat at that table. I know what it costs in patience, not money.

## What this has to do with accounting

I spent years as a Chartered Accountant before I built anything with code. One thing that trade teaches you: every extra line item is a question somebody has to answer later. Every unexplained step in a process is a liability. A clean ledger has nothing in it that doesn't need to be there.

Software has the same problem, it's just hidden better. Every signup form, every "create an account to continue," every unnecessary click, is a line item nobody reconciles. It's friction dressed up as a feature. Companies call it "onboarding." I call it a tax you pay before you've gotten anything.

The honest version of a tool removes that line item. It gives you the thing first. If the thing is worth coming back for, you'll sign up on your own terms, not because a wall made you.

## What Nova actually does

Nova is a fast AI helper for homework, code, writing and ideas. You can open it and start chatting as a guest, right now, no signup. No form stands between you and the answer to the question you actually came with.

If you're an adult and you want to keep things, you can sign in. That saves your chats, your projects, and gives Nova memory of what you've worked on before, so you're not re-explaining the same assignment three nights in a row. But that's a choice you make after you've already gotten value, not a toll you pay before it.

It's in free beta. Homework help, code help, writing help, or just a place to think out loud about an idea that's stuck in your head. The guest door is open. The saved-memory room is there if you want it.
