## What paperwork and reconciliation have in common

Before any of this, I was a Chartered Accountant. I closed books. And I want to tell you what a compliance file actually is, because I think it will change how you feel about it.

A DQ file is a reconciliation.

In accounting, reconciliation means: does this match that. Does the bank statement match the ledger. Does what actually happened match what your records say happened. When they match, you can sleep. When they do not, you have a problem you have not found yet, and it is growing quietly in the dark.

Your compliance file is the same shape. On one side, reality: you are a legal, qualified, insured driver right now, today. On the other side, the record that proves it. The whole job of the file is to make those two things match. A lapsed medical card does not mean you are unsafe. It means reality and the record have drifted apart. And an audit is nothing more than a stranger checking whether they still match.

Accountants learned something the hard way about reconciliation: you cannot do it once a year. If you wait for the audit, you are reconstructing history under pressure, and history does not want to be reconstructed. You do it continuously, in small pieces, so the match never breaks in the first place.

That is the shift. Compliance is not a folder you build the night before. It is a match you keep intact every day. And keeping a match intact is exactly the kind of boring, high-stakes, low-glory work that a machine should be doing for you, not you for it.
